01 / Finding

Why these names look invented

Two batches were written by hand, in the register you asked for. The first was 256 names of the Vantor / Novira / Solvara / Elaris kind. The second was 203 respellings of real words, the pattern behind Figma, Vercel and Prysm — Vertyx, Quantiq, Zenyth, Matryx, Kortex.

Four of those 459 were available. Under one percent. Every name a person would write down as obviously good is already registered — usually parked, not used. That is the single most useful fact in this document, because it explains the shape of everything below.

So the search moved to the part of the sound-space nobody farms: Latinate and French endings — -ique, -ade, -une, -ance, -era, -ira — bolted onto short technical roots. Of 1,723 built that way, 652 had a free .com.

That is why every name here reads as European rather than Californian. It is not a style preference; it is where the vacancies are. It also happens to suit an agency selling to Western clients — it sounds like a studio, not a startup.

Also tried and abandoned: 6,700 randomly generated pronounceable words. 85% had a free .com and essentially none were worth saying aloud — availability and brandability are close to inversely correlated, which is the whole problem.

02 / Shortlist

Ten names, ranked

01

Axiune

axi-  axiom, axis
-une  open Latinate close

AK-see-oon

axiune.com.io.dev

Why choose it

An axiom is the thing you accept first and build everything else on — the exact claim a firm selling both products and the services around them wants to make. The same root carries axis: the line a system turns on. It is the only name here whose meaning survives being explained in a single sentence, which matters more than it sounds: a name you can justify in one breath gets repeated correctly by other people.

As a mark

Four vowels in six letters give it an open, unhurried sound and a logotype with plenty of counter-space. The initial A is the most structurally interesting letter to build an icon from — two strokes and a crossbar read as an axis at any size.

Watch out

The -une ending is rare in English, so people will type Axion or Axiom. Both are long registered, so you cannot buy the misspellings — budget for spelling it aloud on every call.

02

Arcique

arc-  arc, trajectory
-ique  technique, unique

ar-SEEK

arcique.com.io.dev unverified

Why choose it

The -ique ending carries technique and unique without having to say either word, and an arc is the actual shape of a project: brief, build, launch, curve away. The most design-forward name on the list — it reads as a studio with a point of view rather than a development shop, and it will sit comfortably beside European agency names on a client's shortlist.

As a mark

Hands you a literal logo: a single drawn arc. Almost no other name here comes with its own geometry already decided.

Watch out

Two plausible readings — ar-SEEK and AR-kick. Pick one and use it in your own voice consistently from day one; the second reading dies out quickly if you never use it. The .dev lookup timed out and needs re-running.

03

Quanira

quan-  quanta, quantify
-ira  soft vowel close

kwa-NEER-a

quanira.com.io.dev unverified

Why choose it

A quantum is the smallest indivisible unit of a thing — a decent description of a plugin, and a good one for a company that sells software in pieces. The kw- opening is genuinely rare in software branding, so it stands out immediately in a list otherwise full of Vs, Zs and Ns.

As a mark

The softest and most premium-sounding name here. It ages well — there is no era-specific technology reference in it to date the company in five years, which is not true of anything built on cyber-, -ify or -AI.

Watch out

Seven letters and three syllables — the slowest to type of the top three. The -ira ending reads slightly pharmaceutical, which is fine for a design-led studio and less so if you later pivot to infrastructure.

04

Orbade

orb-  orbit, sphere
-ade  cascade, decade

or-BAYD

orbade.com.io.dev

Why choose it

Shortest and cleanest on the list at six letters. Orb is a whole system seen from outside; -ade is the suffix of things that unfold over time — cascade, decade. Together they say scale and duration, which is the pitch for a maintenance retainer rather than a one-off build.

As a mark

The best logotype prospect here. Six letters, near-symmetrical, and one strong O that works as a standalone icon down to 16px — which is the size that actually matters, because it is a favicon, an app icon and a marketplace avatar.

Watch out

Bade is the past tense of bid, so a few readers will parse it as "or bade". Minor, and it disappears once the word is set as a logotype.

05

Zephune

zeph-  zephyr, west wind
-une  open Latinate close

ZEF-yoon

zephune.com.io.dev unverified

Why choose it

A zephyr is a light west wind — speed without force. If the agency's pitch is fast delivery and low friction, no other root on this list says it as economically. The Z opening is the rarest first letter in English commerce and the most memorable.

As a mark

Alphabetically last, which is a small but real advantage in any directory that sorts A–Z: you are the name still on screen when someone scrolls to the bottom. The ph also gives the logotype an unusual internal rhythm to work with.

Watch out

Zeph only means something once the zephyr connection is made — unexplained it is just letters. Also the highest misspelling risk here (zeff-, zef-). .dev unverified.

06

Lyrera

lyr-  Lyra, lyric
-era  age, epoch

ly-RAIR-uh

lyrera.com.io.dev unverified

Why choose it

Lyra is a northern constellation and the root of lyric; -era is an age. The most musical name on the list, and the rolled double-r survives being said in Bangla as cleanly as in English — worth weighing when your team says the name far more often than your clients do.

As a mark

Fits a studio that leads with visual craft. Lyra's five stars are a ready-made constellation motif that avoids the obvious swoosh.

Watch out

Half its letters are l/r, which some accents blur into each other. Close enough to Lyra and Riviera that a trademark check should come before you print anything.

07

Onyxique

onyx-  black gemstone
-ique  technique, unique

on-ix-EEK

onyxique.com.io.dev

Why choose it

The most visual root on the list — onyx is a black banded gemstone, so the name arrives with a palette and a material already attached. Paired with -ique it reads as a luxury digital studio, which is the right register if you intend to price a theme catalogue above the marketplace average rather than at it.

As a mark

Black, high-gloss, sharp-edged — an art direction that writes itself and is unusually cheap to execute consistently across a site, a marketplace listing and a deck.

Watch out

Eight letters and three syllables makes it the least typeable name here. It needs a short logotype (a single O) doing the work in every small placement. Onyx alone is also a crowded trademark field — check carefully.

08

Myrance

myr-  myriad, countless
-ance  resonance, radiance

MY-rance

myrance.com.io.dev

Why choose it

Myriad means countless, and -ance turns a root into a quality — resonance, radiance, cadence. The result is the only name here that already sounds like an existing English abstract noun, so it passes as an established company with no explanation at all. That is worth a great deal in a cold email.

As a mark

Reads calm and established rather than new, which suits a firm selling retainers. It is also the easiest name here for a non-technical client to spell correctly on the first try.

Watch out

Said quickly it can land as "my rants". It also sits near Myriad and Mylan, both real companies — of everything on this list, this is the one to run through a trademark search first.

09

Cyrave

cyr-  cyber, Cyrus
-ave  wave, octave

sy-RAYV

cyrave.com.io.dev

Why choose it

The most energetic name on the list. There is an audible wave in it, and the cy- opening carries just enough of cyber to place the company in technology without dating it the way the full prefix would. Right if the agency's voice is young and the products are consumer-facing rather than enterprise.

As a mark

Short, front-loaded and fast to say — it works as a spoken brand, which matters if you expect to be recommended in Slack channels and Facebook groups rather than found in search.

Watch out

The same energy is the risk: rave is plainly visible inside it and reads as nightlife to some audiences. The cy- also invites a "sigh-rave" first reading.

10

Vyrine

vyr-  invented, vir-adjacent
-ine  chlorine, adrenaline

VY-reen

vyrine.com.io.dev

Why choose it

The sleekest and most product-like name here. -ine is the suffix English uses for substances and compounds — chlorine, adrenaline, crystalline — so it reads as a material rather than a company, which is genuinely apt for a business whose output is components other people build with.

As a mark

Six letters, one dominant V, no ascender clutter. Of the ten, it would make the tightest wordmark.

Watch out

This is why it is tenth and not higher: vyr- sits one letter from vir-, as in virus and viral. For a software company that association is worth taking seriously rather than hoping nobody makes it.

04 / Method

How availability was checked

Every candidate was queried against the authoritative registry over RDAP, not a reseller's search box — Verisign for .com, Identity Digital for .io, Google Registry for .dev. HTTP 404 means no registration record exists; 200 means taken.

Going direct avoids the two failure modes of typing names into a registrar: cached results, and search-leak front-running, where a name you looked up is registered by someone else hours later. Four .dev lookups timed out; those are marked unverified above rather than guessed at.

Not covered. No trademark search — not USPTO, not WIPO, not the Bangladesh DPDT register. No social handle check on X, GitHub, npm or LinkedIn. A free domain is necessary but not sufficient: a registered mark in your class can stop you using a name whose domain you own.

Availability decays. Results are from 2 September 2026. A clustered, thematically obvious list like this one is exactly what drop-catchers watch for.

05 / Next

Before you commit

  1. Register your top two or three today. About $12 a year each. Deciding between names you already own beats deciding between names someone else may take this week — and after this search, that is not a theoretical risk.
  2. Take the phone test. Say the name and the domain to someone who has never seen it written, and ask them to type it. Zephune and Arcique are most likely to fail this; Orbade and Myrance least.
  3. Trademark-search in your real classes. Class 42 covers software design and development services; class 9 covers downloadable software. Run Myrance and Onyxique first — both sit near existing marks.
  4. Check the handles you will actually use. GitHub organisation, npm scope, WordPress.org plugin author slug, X, LinkedIn. For a WordPress product business the WordPress.org author slug matters more than X does.
  5. Buy the near-miss for the winner only. vyrera.com if you take Lyrera, quanura.com if you take Quanira, onyxade.com if you take Onyxique. Redirect them; never build on them.
